# Calibration

## There is one dial.

`KnowledgeConfig::knowledge_rate` — the clock. How fast a society accumulates learning.
Set it with `calib_rate.sh`, which bisects it against ONE anchor date (the Bronze Age at
3300 BCE) and then reports where the other six eras land. Those six are the model's own
answer, not targets.

Nothing else in the climb is fitted.

## What the other numbers are

**The era ladder** (`knowledge_to_advance` in `packages/base_game/eras/eras.csv`) is
CONTENT: the running total of the technology tree's own weights, regenerated by
`set_content_exponent.py`. A unit test asserts eras.csv against the tree, so editing the
tree and forgetting to regenerate fails the build rather than drifting quietly.

**The knowledge axis** is measured in technique-equivalents, defined by
`TechnologyAdoptionConfig::difficulty_knowledge_exponent`: each unit of a node's authored
difficulty stands for `exp(exponent)` times the learning of the one below it. Everything
else on that axis (the Boserup soil term, the carrying-ceiling halfsat, the Jones
discovery brake, the printing halfsat) is a position on the ladder and is regenerated
with it.

## What this replaced, and why

`calib_seq.sh` fitted SEVEN era thresholds so that one world hit seven historical dates.
It could not be wrong about any of them, which is another way of saying it tested nothing;
and worse, the era thresholds are the one quantity the grounding doctrine permits as a
pure pacing dial — "they label a trajectory, they do not shape it" — so making them the
definition of the knowledge unit made every constant expressed in that unit float with the
fit. Anchoring a technique's cost on them then stopped the calibration converging at all,
which is how it was caught.

An era is a set of techniques now. A society moves past one when it has worked out enough
of that era's MAIN PATH, which carries both knowing it and having built for it. How long
it takes over that is its own business: a world that runs its main line early and one that
spends three thousand years deepening its side branches are both playing properly, and the
`[pacing]` gate asserts the SHAPE of a climb — order, no skips, nothing free — and nothing
about its speed.

## Running it

    bash tools/calibration/calib_rate.sh            # the clock
    python3 tools/calibration/set_content_exponent.py 1.1   # regenerate the ladder

Both rebuild as they go. Check `[pacing]` afterwards.
